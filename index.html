Every vendor speaks a different dialect

          
EPIC speaks HL7 with Z-segments. The lab speaks ORU. The province wants FHIR with CA-Core profiles, the payer wants X12, the specialist's office sends a fax-turned-CSV — and the mental-health program reports to OMHRS on its own schedule.

Legacy engines charge rent on your own data

          
Interface engines you can't inspect, priced per interface, deployed by consultants, upgradeable only by the vendor. Twenty years of hospital IT says the same thing: the engine must live inside your network, under your keys.

How it works

    
One pipeline. Any protocol in, any protocol out.


    

    

      

        
04

        

          
Universal adapters, not vendor lock-in

          
Raw TCP/IP with any framing, ODBC against any DSN through a bridge sidecar, scheduled API polling, Excel workbooks over base64 — if a system can emit or accept bytes, the engine can interface with it. VPN or site-to-site tunnels need zero engine configuration.

        

      

      

        
05

        

          
Imaging, not just messages

          
Modality Worklist SCP, a Storage SCP receiving from modalities, and C-STORE SCU pushing imaging objects to PACS — the DICOM surface that separates enterprise engines from message movers.

        

      

      

        
06

        

          
AI that assists, on your terms

          
Describe a mapping in plain language and get a sandboxed transform script. Bring your own LLM key — including a private Azure OpenAI inside your own cloud tenant — or run on the engine key, metered per tenant. Every generated script is validated; anything unsafe falls back to a deterministic offline synthesizer.

Straight answers

    
What hospital IT asks us.

    

      

        Does the engine need internet access?
        
No. It installs and runs fully inside your network, including the first install. The only outbound connections it ever makes are the destinations your team configures — your EHR endpoints, your lab feeds, your own mail server for alerts. There is no license server and no telemetry.

      

      

        Where does our data go?
        
Nowhere you don't put it. Messages are stored on your volume, the audit trail is a local append-only log, and imaging lands in your PACS. For Ontario deployments that means PHI never leaves your custodianship — a PHIPA conversation becomes a short one.

      

      

        What happens if HealthTrust IQ disappears?
        
The engine keeps running — that's the architectural point. No activation, no renewal check, no kill switch. Customers additionally receive source-code access for continuity review, so your team is never held hostage by our balance sheet.

      

      

        Does it really cover EPIC, MEDITECH, and the Ontario EMRs?
        
Yes — with named, maintained adaptor profiles rather than generic promises: EPIC ADT/ORU/SIU inbound, MEDITECH Expanse, and the OntarioMD-certified clinic EMRs (Accuro, TELUS PS Suite, OSCAR Pro, Ocean). Anything with an API, a database, or a file drop can be interfaced through the universal adapters.

      

      

        Is the Canada Conformance Accelerator an official certification?
        
No. It produces repeatable structural-readiness results and non-PHI evidence using versioned packs and synthetic fixtures. It does not confer certification, connect to an official validator, or replace onboarding and validation with the relevant external authority. It helps your team find and fix structural issues before those external steps.

      

      

        How is it licensed?
        
Annual subscription per deployment (or perpetual with maintenance), priced by edition — Hospital, Clinic, or Hosted. Source is available to customers for security and continuity review. No per-interface pricing: an engine that charges rent per interface is the disease, not the cure.

      

      

        Can we try it before we buy?
        
Yes — request the bundle and we'll provision access to the private distribution channel, typically the same day. Most teams have it processing test messages inside an hour; a pilot against a real interface follows whenever you're ready.
